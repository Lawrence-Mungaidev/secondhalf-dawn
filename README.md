# Secondhalf Dawn

Build a one-page marketing website for "secondhalf", a Kenyan tech company that builds 

custom backend systems and business-process automation for SMEs (not a generic web 

design agency — website builds are a secondary offering, not the headline).

BRAND

- Name: secondhalf (always lowercase in the wordmark)

- Tagline: "technology, built together."

- Brand philosophy: a turning point — it's not over, whatever came before, this is a 

  new dawn. Confident and warm, not sentimental or corporate.

- Logo concept: a horizon line with a rising sun and fanning rays. Use it as a small 

  mark next to the wordmark in the nav, and again, larger, as a quiet motif in the 

  footer — not repeated as decoration throughout the page.

COLOR (use with restraint — do not turn this into a cream-and-coral SaaS template)

- Ink navy #1C2541 — primary text, primary backgrounds for high-contrast sections

- Coral #E8623D — the one accent color, used sparingly (one CTA, the sun mark, one 

  underline or divider treatment) — never as a background wash or gradient

- Cream #F5F1E8 — light section backgrounds

- Slate gray #6B7280 — secondary text, captions

Do not use a serif display font paired with this palette — that combination reads as 

an AI-generated default. Instead pick a confident, slightly technical sans-serif for 

headings (something with real character — e.g. a grotesque like Söhne, General Sans, 

or similar — not Inter or a default system font) paired with a clean, highly legible 

sans for body copy. Two weights only: one for headings, one for body. No italics, no 

single-word color/bold accents inside headlines, no ALL-CAPS labels above sections.

STRUCTURE (single page, sections below — no identical rounded cards with matching 

drop shadows for every content block; vary the layout section to section)

1. Hero

   Full-height, ink navy background. The horizon/sunrise mark rendered large and 

   subtle (low opacity or line-art) behind or beside the headline rather than as a 

   literal centered logo. Headline should say what we do in plain language — 

   something like: "We build the systems running quietly behind your business." 

   One-line subhead naming the two capabilities: custom software + automation. 

   One primary CTA: "Start a project" or "Talk to us" — no arrow glyph appended to 

   the button text.

2. What we do

   Three offerings, NOT as three identical cards in a row. Give each its own visual 

   weight and treatment based on priority: automation and custom systems are the 

   lead (larger, more space, real explanation of what problem they solve — e.g. 

   "we connect the tools you already use so work happens automatically" for 

   automation), and web development is presented as a smaller, secondary offering 

   underneath. Write plain-language descriptions of outcomes, not feature lists.

3. What we're building

   A section showing the two flagship products in active development: a 

   multi-tenant booking and scheduling system for service businesses, and a 

   rent/tenant management system for landlords with M-Pesa-based rent collection. 

   Frame these honestly as "in development" / "coming soon" — don't fake screenshots 

   or fabricate customer logos. A short line on a third product to come, described 

   as automation-driven, without overspecifying it.

4. How we work / who we are

   Two co-founders, one focused on backend systems and software architecture, one 

   focused on automation and workflow engineering (n8n). Write this as a short, 

   human paragraph about the combination of skills, not a formal bio card with 

   headshots and job titles in a grid.

5. Contact

   Simple, direct: an email link and a short line inviting outreach. No contact form 

   complexity needed for v1 — a mailto link and WhatsApp link are enough.

Footer: small logo mark, tagline, copyright, no extra link clutter.

MOTION

One deliberate moment only — e.g. the sun/horizon mark animating gently into place 

on page load, or the rays extending once. No fade-up-on-scroll applied to every 

section, no hover-lift on every card — that pattern reads as generated.

TONE OF COPY

Plain, confident, second-person where natural ("we build the systems running behind 

your business"), no marketing filler like "unlock", "empower", "seamless", 

"cutting-edge". Sentence case throughout, no tracked-out capital labels.

Fully responsive, mobile-first given most visitors will be on phones in Kenya. 

Fast-loading, no unnecessary large hero images or stock photography — the sunrise 

mark and typography should carry the visual identity on their own.
